The era translator answers the bar-argument question: was a 2019 season better than a 2024 one? It rates every driver-season by the same yardstick, how far ahead of (or behind) his teammate the driver was in the same car, and presents it as a leaderboard. Because both teammates share the car, the car cancels, and so does the 2022 ground-effect regulation change, which moved both teammates alike. That is what puts 2018 and 2025 on one scale; no era offset is applied. Negative means faster than his teammate.

## What it shows

Each row is one driver-season rating:

- **Rating (gap to teammate)**: per race, the median of the driver's lap-by-lap gap to his teammate on laps both ran clean; averaged over the season and shrunk toward the season mean with a 5-race prior. Negative = faster than his teammate(s), in seconds per lap. Most seasons sit within ±0.4s.
- **95% CI**: the season rating's posterior uncertainty. A wider interval means fewer races.
- **★**: drivers with at least 8 races on each side of the 2022 regulation boundary. Shown for context; no rating depends on them.

This is the same underlying data as the [era ratings timeline](/app/era-ratings-timeline), presented as a season leaderboard.

<Note>
  A minimum of 5 races is required for a driver-season to appear, so part-season cameos are excluded.
</Note>

## How to use it

1. **Select a season** to rank that year, or compare ratings across seasons on the shared scale.
2. **Check who the teammate was** before comparing two rows: the rating is a margin over that teammate.
3. **Read the CI** before ranking two adjacent rows; overlapping intervals are a statistical tie.
4. **Export the data** to build your own cross-era comparisons.

## Reading the signal correctly

The rating is **teammate-relative**: it answers "how far ahead of the driver in the same car was he?", not "how far ahead of the field?" and not "what lap time would he set today?". A −0.3s season next to a weak teammate and a −0.3s season next to a strong one are not the same achievement.

<Warning>
  Until 2026-09-28 this page applied a "bridge driver" era offset to every pre-2022 season. It was not statistically distinguishable from zero and it inverted the gap it was meant to remove, so it was dropped (audit finding F45). The per-race statistic also changed then, from a best-laps ceiling against the teammate's typical lap to a like-for-like lap-by-lap median (F40). See the [decomposition limitations](/decomposition/limitations).
</Warning>

## Data source

The chart queries `int_era_normalized_driver_rating` via DuckDB-Wasm in the browser.

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `era_adjusted_rating` | Season rating: gap to teammate, seconds (negative = faster). Carries no era offset |
| `era_adjusted_rating_ci_low_s` / `era_adjusted_rating_ci_high_s` | 95% credible interval |
| `bridge_driver_anchor_flag` | 8+ races on each side of 2022 (the ★); context only |

The data window covers **2018–2025**.

For the full model definition, see the [`int_era_normalized_driver_rating` reference](/reference/models/int/int_era_normalized_driver_rating).
