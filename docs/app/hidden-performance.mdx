A points-less afternoon can hide a great drive. Hidden performance surfaces those: it compares each driver's ghost-projected finish what the model expected given their pace in a chosen host car against where they actually finished. A driver who was projected higher than they finished was, in pace terms, under-rewarded by the result.

## What it shows

The table ranks drivers by the gap between projection and result:

- **Ghost-projected position (`fct_ghost_race_finish`)**: each driver's lap times recombined with a chosen host constructor's car pace, then re-ranked by predicted mean fuel-adjusted lap pace (not cumulative race time, so a driver who ran fewer laps is not ranked artificially high).
- **Delta**: projected minus actual finish. A *negative* delta means the model projected a better finish than actually occurred the driver was under-rewarded.
- **Confidence**: how well the host constructor's pace is estimated from panel data; more races with that team means higher confidence.

<Note>
  Only scenarios where `avg_recombination_confidence ≥ 0.3` are included, and short runs with too few laps for a reliable estimate are excluded.
</Note>

## How to use it

1. **Pick a host constructor** from the filter bar; projections re-score against that car.
2. **Sort by delta** to surface the most under-rewarded drives.
3. **Read confidence** before trusting a large delta a low-confidence host pace makes the projection noisier.
4. **Use the self-scenario check** as a sanity test: when the host equals the driver's own team, projected per-lap pace should equal actual per-lap pace. Projected finish position will usually differ from actual finish position.

## Reading the signal correctly

The delta is **pace versus result**, not an injustice ledger strategy, safety cars, and incidents legitimately move finishing positions in ways a pace projection cannot see. A negative delta says "faster than the result", not "robbed".

<Warning>
  When the host constructor equals the driver's own team (`is_self_scenario`), the predicted mean lap time (`predicted_mean_lap_s`) equals the actual mean lap time (`actual_mean_lap_s`). A gap between those two lap times in a self-scenario row signals a data issue. The projected finish position (`predicted_finish_position`) does not have to equal the actual finish position (`actual_finish_position`) in a self-scenario row. The projected finish position ranks every driver in the scenario by mean fuel-adjusted lap pace in the same car. The actual finish position also reflects strategy, safety cars, incidents and retirements. So a non-zero `delta_vs_actual_position` in a self-scenario row is expected and is not a data issue. On the 2018–2025 build, `delta_vs_actual_position` is non-zero in about 95% of self-scenario rows (n = 2,786 self-scenario rows that have an actual finish position).
</Warning>

## Data source

The chart queries `fct_ghost_race_finish` via DuckDB-Wasm in the browser.

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `predicted_finish_position` | Ghost-projected finishing position in the host car |
| `actual_finish_position` | The real finishing position |
| `delta_vs_actual_position` | Projected minus actual; negative = under-rewarded; NULL when the driver has no finishing position |
| `avg_recombination_confidence` | Panel-data confidence; gated at `≥ 0.3` |

The data window covers **2018–2025**.

For the full model definition, see the [`fct_ghost_race_finish` reference](/reference/models/fct/fct_ghost_race_finish).
