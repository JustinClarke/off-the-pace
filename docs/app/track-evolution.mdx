A circuit is not a fixed stopwatch: field pace usually drifts over a race, and the ambient conditions drift underneath the cars. Track evolution decomposes the lap-time baseline into those two time-varying components, a linear pace trend and an ambient drift, both split out of the field pace baseline (`field_pace_smoothed_s`, carried onto each lap as `base_track_pace_s`). Because they live inside the baseline every lap is measured against, they are informational and are not terms of the additive identity. It separates a change in field pace from a change in any single car's pace. The pace trend is usually read as rubber build, but it is not a direct measurement of rubber: it also absorbs anything else that moves field pace steadily.

## What it shows

The chart splits the moving baseline into two curves plus a weather overlay:

- **Pace trend (`rubber_component_s`)**: the fitted straight-line change in fuel- and compound-corrected field pace over the race, relative to the race mean, from one slope per race capped at zero. Positive means slower than the race mean (early laps); negative means faster (late laps). Where a race has too few laps for a fit the value is not measured and the chart shows a gap.
- **Ambient component**: the part of the remaining pace residual that tracks the lap's track temperature, fitted per race on track temperature only (humidity is carried as a column but is not in the fit). Where a race has no usable track-temperature spread the component is 0.0, not a measured zero.
- **Rainfall**: laps tagged with the rainfall flag are highlighted. Wet laps are not removed from the fit, so the single per-race fit does not treat rain as a break. `rain_state` is a separate column carried from the weather reading; a lap with no weather reading is neither wet nor dry (`rain_state` is `NO_WEATHER`).

<Note>
  Both components are fitted on the field pace baseline (`int_field_pace_curve`), not on every lap. That baseline uses laps that are not out-laps, are not the last two valid laps of a non-final stint, are within 107% of the fastest lap at the same lap number, run in free air or a tow, and have a known tyre cost; the middle 80% of those laps is averaged. Safety-car, VSC and red-flag lap numbers are treated as boundaries when the baseline is smoothed, and wet laps are not removed from the fit, so a race with rain mixes dry and wet pace in one trend.
</Note>

## How to use it

1. **Select a race** from the filter bar; the pace-trend and ambient curves load.
2. **Read the pace trend's slope**: a steep fall from slower-than-mean early laps to faster-than-mean late laps means field pace improved strongly over the race, which is commonly (not only) rubber build.
3. **Watch the ambient curve** for evening cool-down or warming that shifts pace independently of the trend.
4. **Note rainfall highlights**: wet laps stay in the fit, so a pace swing around rain reflects wet and dry pace mixed in one trend.

## Reading the signal correctly

These offsets are **field-wide baseline shifts**, not any single car's pace they describe the track everyone shares. The pace trend and ambient drift are estimated from the fuel- and compound-corrected field baseline, so they reflect what's left after fuel and tyre effects are removed.

<Warning>
  On short or interrupted races there may be too few clean laps to resolve the ambient component cleanly, and it can absorb residual pace-trend signal. Treat a noisy ambient curve as low-confidence. See the [decomposition limitations](/decomposition/limitations).
</Warning>

## Data source

The chart queries `int_track_evolution` via DuckDB-Wasm in the browser, for a single race.

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `rubber_component_s` | Linear pace trend vs the race mean (positive = slower than the race mean, negative = faster); NULL where not measured |
| `ambient_component_s` | Track-temperature-driven offset (fitted on track temperature only); 0.0 where the race has no usable temperature fit |
| `rainfall_flag` | Whether the lap was rain-affected (highlighted); NULL where there is no weather reading |
| `lap_number` | The race lap, the x-axis |

This is a **single-race** view across **2018–2025** data. The model applies no year filter of its own. Races with no field pace base have no rows: 2018_1 (Australia) and 2018_2 (Bahrain) have no race telemetry in bronze, so every one of their pace cells is thin and both are excluded from the field base. Only laps with a non-thin field pace cell get a row.

For the full model definition, see the [`int_track_evolution` reference](/reference/models/int/int_track_evolution).
