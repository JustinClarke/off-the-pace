Driver performance in Formula 1 is never a single number. On any lap, a driver's pace reflects simultaneous contributions from car quality, tyre state, fuel load, traffic, and their own skill. This page explains how Off The Pace isolates the driver, and what each of the two ratings means when you encounter it in the app.

## The two ratings

Both are **field-centred**: they measure each driver against the field's median on that lap, removing car, fuel, ambient, and rubber effects that are common to all cars. A driver's rating answers what comparative question?

| Rating | The question it answers | Grain | Sign |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pure pace skill** | With car, tyres, fuel, and traffic equal, how fast is he? | Lap, 5-lap window, stint, race | s/lap, positive = faster |
| **Relative pace vs same-strategy peers** | Against cars on the same compound, tyre age and lap right now, is he quicker? | Lap, race-pair (vs specific peer) | s/lap, positive = faster |

## How each rating is computed

### Tier 1: pure pace skill

A lap's field-centred isolated time removes compound cost and dirty air, leaving:

```
y = lap_time − compound_cost − dirty_air_cost − MEDIAN( lap_time − compound_cost − dirty_air_cost )  [over the field]
```

A two-way fixed-effects fit on `y` with driver and constructor-race effects, run separately for each regulation era (2018–2021 and 2022–2025) on the pre-cliff laps that pure skill is averaged over, isolates the car's contribution to pace. The car term is then subtracted:

```
pace_isolated = −(y − car_effect)           [the negative is a sign convention; positive = faster]
```

On pre-cliff laps, pure skill is his pace on an equal car with field-median compound and tyre degradation. On laps past the tyre cliff, pure skill is the extrapolation of his pre-cliff trend and is flagged as estimated.

**Why the car term is fitted on this panel:** the existing `int_constructor_car_fe` is fitted on pace before compound and traffic are removed, so it absorbs each team's average tyre-strategy choices and traffic exposure. Subtracting it would manufacture a defect (a component inside the term, subtracted again). This car term is fitted on the same scale as pure skill.

### Tier 2: relative pace vs same-strategy peers

For each lap, find every other driver with:
- A clean lap on the same lap number (same fuel, compound, weather)
- The same tyre compound
- Tyre age within ±2 laps of this driver's

Relative pace is raw pace, matched within ±2 laps of tyre age. Compute the lap-time difference to each peer and average across peers:

```
relative_pace = AVG( peer_laptime − my_laptime )  [over peers]
```

No model adjusts for the tyre-age difference between the two cars. The ±2-lap peer match controls for it instead.

The identity below splits this peer-relative gap into driver, car, and traffic:

```
relative_pace = (pace_gap) + (car_advantage) + (traffic_advantage) + (tyre_age_bias)
```

where each term is the difference between the two drivers. `tyre_age_bias` is what the ±2-lap match leaves behind: the compound seed's price for the two cars' tyre-age difference. It is exactly zero when both cars are on the same tyre age, so on those laps the three named terms add up to relative pace exactly. It is not published as a column; see *Limitations* for its size.

**Coverage:** relative is NULL when no peer exists on that lap.

## Confidence and reliability

Each rating at each grain (lap, 5-lap window, stint, race) has:

- **SE:** the standard error of the estimate, computed from sample size, within-driver variance, and autocorrelation
- **λ (reliability):** signal variance / (signal variance + SE²), in [0, 1]. λ expresses how much of the raw estimate is signal vs. noise.
- **Shrinkage:** the published value is λ × raw. A thin estimate (low λ) is pulled toward zero; a large sample (λ → 1) is barely adjusted.
- **confidence_pct = ROUND(100 × λ × method_score)**, where method_score is validated separately (see *Validation* below).

### Sample sizes and effective precision

| Grain | Clean laps | SE | Reliability λ | Trust |
| :--- | --: | --: | --: | --: |
| 5-lap window | 3–5 | 0.21–0.28 s | 0.43–0.56 | ~40–60% signal |
| Stint | ~16 median | 0.12 s | ~0.80 | ~80% signal |
| Race | ~45 | 0.07 s | ~0.92 | ~92% signal |

The 5-lap window is about half signal: it answers "is he on it right now?", not "who is better over a season." Verdicts belong at stint or race grain. Never headline a thin window number; always quote its confidence and position it against stint and race context.

## Validation and method scores

Each rating is validated against pre-registered thresholds on test families:

- **V1 (cross-season stability):** split-half correlation within seasons, adjacent-season Pearson, and movers vs. stayers contrast
- **V2 (confound tests):** within-driver car leakage, compound ordering, fuel-saving stratum, convergent validity against existing skill measures
- **V3 (peer-pair validation):** car and traffic pricing at matched strategy, teammate parity, rolling-origin backtest
- **V4 (physical sanity):** fuel effect in known band, isolation immunity to fuel, compound ordering across the field

Tests are weighted per rating. A **critical** test failure (red-flag defect) sets the rating's method_score to 0 (F grade, suppressed). Otherwise, method_score = weighted average of all tests' pass rates.

| Test | Pure | Relative |
| :-- | :-: | :-: |
| V1 stability | **critical** | — |
| V2 confound | **critical** (V2a) | — |
| V3 peer pair | — | **critical** |
| Others | 1–2 weight | 1–2 weight |

### Grade

- **A** (≥ 0.9): solid method
- **B** (≥ 0.75): good method
- **C** (≥ 0.5): acceptable method
- **F** (< 0.5 or critical fail): suppressed; the rating is not published

### Known issues

#### Pure pace skill: F-grade, publication deferred to post-v15

**Decision (2026-10-03):** Pure pace fails two critical V1 sub-checks (adjacent-season Pearson, movers vs stayers) due to the per-era car-term fit design, not data quality or the driver isolation method itself. The rating is statistically sound within-era (V1a split-half: 0.897, well above 0.60 threshold) and shows no car leakage when confounds are demeaned (V2a: |corr| = 0.054). However, it does not generalise across regulation-era boundaries, and the project's pre-registered critical rule sets any critical failure to grade F, suppressed. This is the correct call: claiming stability across an era boundary we cannot validate is unwise.

**Known failures documented for post-v15 refinement:**

- **Stability across the 2022 rules change (V1b).** Each driver's skill is fitted as one value per regulation era (2018, 2019–2021, 2022–2025), so a driver's pure pace barely moves between seasons inside an era (adjacent-season Pearson 0.82–0.98). Across the 2021→2022 boundary it drops to 0.60. An existing skill measure that does not assume one value per era (`driver_skill_field_s`) shows no drop there (0.91). So the two eras place drivers differently: the car/driver split changes at the boundary, not the drivers. Pure moves most for TSU (+0.53 s), PER (+0.37 s), GAS (+0.26 s) and HAM (+0.19 s), all of whom the era-free measure has flat or slower. **Do not compare a driver's pure pace across the 2021/2022 boundary.** Post-v15 options: (i) adopt the era-free fit (shrinks coverage to 36% of races due to team-island problem); (ii) publish per-era only, with a UI flag; (iii) re-examine the regulation-era boundary definition. All three require design review.

- **Confidence interval width for car leakage (V1c).** Movers' confidence interval [1.043, 2.673] reaches past the 1.5 threshold, so we cannot confidently rule out car leakage from the 1.5× rule. The effect is small in practice (ratio point estimate 1.19), but the formal test fails. The bootstrap did not cluster by driver; post-v15 could refine by driver-era. Currently tracked as a borderline case: "no leakage detected", not "proof of none".

#### Other known failures

These failures are known and tracked for after v15. None of them is fixed here.
- **Compound levels (V2b, V4b, V4c in 2018).** Within the same driver and race, pure should not depend on the compound, but it does. MEDIUM−HARD reads 0.20–0.42 s faster on the Medium in every season, SOFT−HARD up to 0.75 s faster on the Soft, and 2018 SUPERSOFT−MEDIUM 0.91 s faster on the Supersoft. SOFT−MEDIUM flips the other way from 2022 (2024: the Soft reads 0.45 s slower). So the compound seed's levels are off per compound pair, in both directions. Soft tyres usually run the heavy-fuel first stint, so the bias also shows up as a small fuel slope (V4b). The fix is the seed re-estimate (WI-02b).
- **Traffic pricing (V3b).** On laps where only one of the pair is in dirty air, pure reads 0.059 s slower than the same pair's clean-air gap (95% CI 0.031–0.090 s). θ_air slightly under-prices traffic. The effect is small, and it is documented rather than corrected.
- **Absolute fuel cost (V4a).** The implied fuel effect is 0.020 s/kg, below the physical band of 0.025–0.040 s/kg. This affects the absolute decomposition ("fuel cost him X"), not the ratings, which are field-centred and cancel fuel exactly.

## Limitations and blind spots

- **Per-driver fuel strategy:** every car on a lap receives the same fuel cost in the model. A driver told to save fuel reads as lower pure skill. V2d quantifies this stratum; nothing corrects it.
- **Wet races:** excluded by the clean-lap filter. These ratings say nothing about wet-weather skill.
- **Tyre-age bias in relative pace:** relative pace is raw pace, matched within ±2 laps of tyre age, and nothing adjusts for the age difference that remains. Within a few laps of tyre age, a pair's lap-to-lap gap is driven by racing (attacking, defending, the push after a stop) far more than by tyre wear, which is why the method matches on age rather than modelling it. The cost is the `tyre_age_bias` term in the identity. It is zero on the 56% of pair-laps where both cars are on the same tyre age. Over a pair's race it is under 0.01 s for the median pair, but 0.17 s at the 90th percentile. When most of a pair's matched laps are one or two laps apart in tyre age, quote the age spread with the number.
- **Cross-era comparison:** pure pace should not be compared across eras (2018, 2019–2021, 2022–2025). Each era is fitted separately, so a driver's pure value is only comparable with other drivers in the same era. This is a property of the per-era fit, not of the regulations, and no cross-era claim is made.
- **Car identification:** rests on the teammate network and on each driver's skill being one value per regulation era (2018–2021, 2022–2025), not one value for the whole archive. Uncertainty is not propagated into SE; V1c, V2a and V3a test for leakage.
- **Driver-races with no pure rating (390 of 3,102, 12.6%):** this is a limit of the method, not a bug. Pure skill needs a car term, and the car term is identified only through drivers who changed teams. `car_term_source` says why a row has none:
  - `unidentified` (263 driver-races): the team is an island. Its drivers drove for no other team in that era, so no chain of team-changers links its car to the rest of the field. This covers Haas 2018–2021 (139; GRO, MAG, FIT, MAZ, MSC), Alfa Romeo 2022–2023 (78) and Kick Sauber 2024 (46; BOT, ZHO).
  - `not_estimated` (127 driver-races): only one of the team's two drivers has clean pre-cliff laps in that race, so that race cannot separate him from the car.

  Rankings leave these driver-races out rather than guess, and a missing race is not a zero. Relative pace needs no car term, so it still covers them (262 of the 263 island driver-races have one). Bridging the islands through drivers who cross the 2022 boundary (MAG and MSC for Haas, BOT for Sauber) would bring back the assumption that a driver's skill is constant from 2018 to 2025, so it is not done.
- **Season levels:** pure skill is centred on each lap's whole field, including laps on tyres past their cliff, which currently read fast. Seasons with many such laps sit lower as a whole. In 2018, 42% of clean laps are cliff laps, and its mean race pure sat at −0.45 s, against −0.03 to −0.12 s in 2019–2024. `pure_season_offset_gain_s` adds **+0.37 s** to every 2018 pure value (the 2018 baseline offset), which puts 2018 at the other seasons' level (−0.08 s). The offset is one constant for the whole season, so it changes no ranking or gap inside 2018. 2025 still sits low (−0.25 s) and has no offset. Treat any cross-season comparison of pure skill as measured with a caveat. The real fix is the compound seed's cliff cost (WI-02b); the offset is removed once that lands.
- **Car degradation character:** pooled from both drivers in each team, so a driver far better at tyre management than his teammate is shrunk toward him. The AKM decomposition (03c roadmap item) is the fix.
- **2018 traffic:** about 9% of 2018 laps have no telemetry and are coded clear air. `D = 0` on those laps biases 2018 pure skill upward for drivers who were actually in traffic.
- **Rolling windows:** the car term is fitted on all races (retrospective), and `m_s` uses the whole stint. Nothing here is live-safe yet; that is follow-on work.

## Example: VER vs PER, 2023 Saudi Arabian GP (same car)

This worked example demonstrates the identity and shows what "same car" means in tier 3:

**Setup:** Verstappen started 15th, Pérez 2nd. On the 37 matched laps (same compound, tyre age within ±2), VER was 0.46 s/lap slower than PER on raw pace.

**The question:** is VER's deficit a pace gap, a car gap, or traffic?

**The answer (the identity):**
```
relative_pace = −0.459 s/lap
  = (pace gap) + (car advantage) + (traffic advantage) + (tyre-age bias)
  = −0.370     +  0.000          + −0.089              +  0.000
     [VER's isolated pace was 0.37 s slower; same car, so 0.00;
      dirty air cost VER 0.09 s a lap more than PER; same tyre age on every matched lap, so 0.00]
```

The car term is identically zero: both drivers are in the same car, so the constructor-race effect cancels. Most of VER's deficit on these laps sits in the pace gap, and traffic explains about a fifth of it.

## Worked examples (additional)

### 2021 Styrian GP (VER–HAM)

VER's relative pace against HAM on matched laps (raw pace, same compound, tyre age within ±2): **+0.233 s/lap** over **54 matched laps**.
Expected (probe): +0.24 s/lap over 57 laps. **Reproduced within tolerance.**

The identity splits it as a pace gap of +0.44 s/lap for VER, a car term of −0.23 s/lap (the Mercedes read faster), traffic of +0.01 s/lap and a tyre-age bias of +0.004 s/lap (the two cars' tyres were one lap apart on every matched lap). VER's edge on matched laps is his pace, partly offset by the slower car.

### 2021 São Paulo GP (HAM–VER)

HAM's relative pace against VER on matched laps (raw pace, same compound, tyre age within ±2): **−0.041 s/lap** over **18 matched laps**, so VER was 0.041 s a lap quicker.
The WI doc's probe had +0.05 s/lap over 41 laps. Matching within ±2 laps of tyre age halves the sample: the 18 laps a ±3 match would add are all three laps apart. Read the result as level. Lap 2 alone (HAM 0.995 s slower) carries the sign, and the other 17 laps average +0.015 s for HAM.

The identity splits it as a car term of +0.67 s/lap in HAM's favour, a pace gap of −0.52 s/lap (VER's isolated pace was faster), traffic of −0.18 s/lap against HAM and a tyre-age bias of −0.007 s/lap. On matched laps the model places HAM's strength in the car, not in his pace. The case study at `docs/findings/sao-paulo-2021.mdx` reads the race as "brilliant driving and brilliant strategy", using the seven-term model on raw lap times. This split does not support its driving half, and that page has not yet been reconciled with it.

### 2023 Saudi Arabian GP (VER–PER, same car)

VER's relative pace against PER on matched laps: **−0.459 s/lap** over **37 matched laps**.
The WI doc's probe had −0.40 s/lap over 40 laps; the current value is 0.059 s/lap outside that tolerance.

Both drivers were in the same car, so the car advantage is zero by construction. The deficit splits as a pace gap of −0.37 s/lap and traffic of −0.09 s/lap (PER started 2nd, VER 15th). See the worked example above.

## How to use these ratings

- **On the app:** every rating carries its `confidence_pct` (as a visual confidence bar) and `trust_label` (solid / indicative / thin / suppress). Never quote a `suppress` rating or one with < 50% confidence; prefer stint or race grain for verdicts.
- **In race-week tools:** the 5-lap window is useful for live commentary (flagging form changes), but always position it against the stint aggregate.
- **For analysis:** use the identity to decompose a gap into driver, car, and traffic. Cite the peer count and age spread alongside the number.
- **Sign convention:** positive = faster / better throughout. A pure skill of +0.15 s means 0.15 s a lap faster than the field.

## Data source

Both ratings are computed on every clean lap in the archive (2018–2025). Clean laps:
- Have `correction_weight = 1.0` (fully clean)
- Are not pit-in, pit-out, or rain laps
- Are not part of a safety car period
- Have valid compound and tyre-age signals
- Have at least 8 clean laps in their (race, lap_number) cell (field size gate)

Measured coverage on the 2018–2025 archive:
- Pure skill: 96.6% of eligible laps (87.4% of driver-races; see *Limitations* for the rest)
- Relative: 86.6% of eligible laps (coverage limited by peer availability on the same compound within ±2 laps of tyre age)

The two ratings are published in:
- `fct_driver_isolation_lap`: lap-level values, 5-lap windows (trailing)
- `fct_driver_isolation_stint`: stint-phase aggregates (early / mid / cliff / recovery / all)
- `fct_driver_isolation_race`: race-level aggregates

They feed into the LLM query agent and (once validated) into the race-week live tool and app publication.
