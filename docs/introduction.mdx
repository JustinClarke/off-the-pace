Off The Pace answers a question F1 commentary almost never does: when a car is off the pace, *exactly* why? A single lap time conflates fuel weight, tyre wear, track temperature, the car's own structural speed, the aerodynamic penalty of following another car, and the driver's own execution all in one number. Off The Pace separates them, attributing every lap to its causes in seconds, not adjectives.

## The core question

A driver who looks slow on lap 40 might be managing a tyre cliff, carrying dirty air from the car ahead, or simply running heavier fuel than the rest of the field. Any of those causes would make a fast driver look slow, and a single lap time can't tell them apart. Off The Pace can: if the dirty-air component reads `+0.4 s` on lap 32, the car lost 400 ms to aerodynamic wake on that lap, full stop.

## Seven causes, one identity

Every lap's pace, relative to the field, is explained by seven named components fuel load, tyre compound wear, track rubber, ambient temperature, the constructor's car, dirty air, and the driver's own skill and those seven always sum to the observed gap exactly, not approximately. That additive identity is the foundation the whole project is built on: it's a CI-enforced contract, tested on every lap of every race, not a claim made in prose. The full equation, every coefficient, and how each term is estimated live on the [Transform tab](/decomposition/seven-term-identity) this page stays at the level of *what* each term means.

| Term | What it captures |
|---|---|
| **Fuel** | Weight penalty from remaining fuel mass positive and decreasing through the race |
| **Compound** | Tyre degradation, steepening sharply after the tyre cliff |
| **Rubber** | Track evolution as rubber builds on the racing line; typically faster over a session |
| **Ambient** | Track temperature deviation from session baseline |
| **Constructor** | The car's structural pace relative to the field, independent of the driver |
| **Dirty air** | Pace penalty for running in another car's aerodynamic wake |
| **Driver skill** | What's left once the other six are subtracted the driver's own contribution |

## Data coverage and the app

import OverviewNumbers from '/snippets/overview-numbers.mdx';

<OverviewNumbers />

Timing data comes from FastF1; standings and pit-stop reference data come from Jolpica, the Ergast-compatible successor (see [Data sources](/data/overview)). The browser app at [off-the-pace.web.app](https://off-the-pace.web.app) surfaces the full mart no server, no compute cost. DuckDB-Wasm runs SQL in WebAssembly directly in your browser, with queries completing in milliseconds against the gold Parquet files; the trained tyre models run alongside it via ONNX Runtime Web.

<Note>
  Off The Pace attributes lap times that *already happened* it is not a lap-time prediction model. The ML layer predicts tyre cliff onset and degradation rate, which the simulator uses to project forward not a substitute for the historical decomposition.
</Note>
